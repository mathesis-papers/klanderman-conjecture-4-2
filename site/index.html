Correcting Klanderman et al.'s isolated-top transfer-system conjecture via DeMark et al.'s tulip theorem

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Published paper · Version 2 · CC BY 4.0

PDFSourceCitationRepositoryMathesis recordLean source

Abstract

Conjecture 4.2 of Klanderman, Lewis, Monson, Shibata and Van Niel, in Characterizing Transfer Systems for Non-Abelian Groups, arXiv:2511.13439v2, Section 4, printed page 15, receives an explicit correction by an elementary derivation from DeMark et al.’s tulip theorem (arXiv:2604.00335v1, 1 April 2026, Proposition 3.17) and the hull theorem of Mazur et al. (2024, Proposition 2.14). Under the conjecture’s two-component hypothesis with isolated top, the transfer system is non-LSP precisely when the group order is composite, and its ordinary generated extension is compatible precisely when the original system is saturated. The non-LSP classification needs only top isolation, not two components; under the original hypotheses, all compatible partners form the lattice of conjugation-invariant order ideals of proper subgroups. On Q8, the system consisting of identities and e to H for every proper subgroup H violates compatibility: the generated extension omits {±1} to <i>. The classification also identifies the least compatible extension containing e to G and separates ordinary transfer generation from compatibility closure.
